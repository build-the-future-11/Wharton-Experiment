## Model contract

An observation row supplies asset total returns, its decision timestamp, and its actual availability timestamp. The decision for row t occurs before that row's return is known. The context is rows `[t-L,t)` and is allowed only if its last observation is strictly available before the decision. Assets and units are fixed by the input manifest, never inferred from column position. A label is the compounded total return over `[t,t+H)`, with maturity equal to the last observation timestamp plus explicit reporting delay. Training and memory eligibility use maturity strictly less than as-of. Defaults retain the recovered context 128, primary horizon 20, and seed 11. The operational horizon is real, not a label on a one-step target.

At each evaluation origin, eligible samples are chronological. The earliest 80% are model-fit candidates and the remainder are calibration/memory observations. Model-fit labels that mature at or after calibration begins are purged. Feature centering and scaling are fit only on the retained training contexts. A GRU plus linear projection maps contexts to 16-dimensional latents. An EMA copy encodes the future return sequence, with no target gradients. A two-layer predictor matches its latent. Training minimizes latent squared error plus 0.1 return-head squared error, 0.1 variance-floor penalty and 0.01 off-diagonal covariance penalty. Adam uses 0.001 learning rate, EMA 0.99, 20 epochs, one CPU thread, deterministic Torch operations, and gradient clipping. Future-sequence targets are used only when their labels have matured within the fit subset.

A separate 32-state leaky tanh reservoir uses normalized returns, leak 0.3, and a recurrent operator norm of 0.8. The norm bound ensures contraction; the `spectral_radius` configuration field is implemented conservatively as an operator-norm ceiling. Each context starts at zero, ensuring inference does not depend on batching or request order. Reservoir state and JEPA latent are concatenated and standardized using fit samples. One XGBoost regressor per asset uses 64 trees, depth 3, learning rate 0.05, and one CPU thread. `head=ridge` is a named ablation with alpha 1, never an automatic fallback.

The last 256 calibration context keys store out-of-fit residuals. Prediction retrieves up to five positive-cosine neighbors, filters their label maturity, and adds 0.25 times their similarity-weighted residual. No query or liability schedule writes memory. The current candidate memory is frozen per fitted checkpoint; updating it requires a new chronological fit. This avoids treating future outcomes as immediately available. The model has no approved predictive advantage.

Historical mean, standardized ridge, and compounded last-return persistence are evaluated alongside the candidate. They use only matured labels at each origin. The strong ridge and historical-mean baselines use all eligible history; the candidate reserves a calibration portion. Additional ridge and raw-XGBoost matched-fit baselines use exactly the candidate head-training rows. Raw XGBoost matches the 64-tree/depth-3 budget, not total end-to-end encoder compute. A memory-off contrast reuses the same trained encoder/reservoir/heads. All row sets and contrasts are retained; none is silently selected as winner. Origins are explicit and test windows must be disjoint (`stride >= horizon`). Metrics are MSE, MAE, and OOS R² versus historical mean. There is no p-value or confirmation claim from a tiny development run. Failed run receipts are retained. No hyperparameter search is automatic.

## Distribution and controller

Historical log-return blocks are sampled with a fixed seed, preserving within-block temporal and cross-asset dependence. Their historical mean is removed and `log1p(horizon_forecast)/H` is added. This conditions expected log growth, not arithmetic expected wealth. Scenarios are finite empirical exploratory draws; tail calibration is unproven. Forecasts <= -1 are rejected rather than silently clipped.

The planner uses one common asset-weight vector for every scenario and all horizon steps. This is a deliberately restricted common-action receding-horizon controller, not a scenario-adaptive policy or a reproduction of frozen M11. It repeatedly simulates the same ledger as the standalone command. Long-only asset weights sum to at most one, with remaining cash explicit. An explicit maximum asset weight applies. SLSQP evaluates both cash and feasible equal-weight starts, retaining the best successful feasible solution. Failure raises; it never substitutes all-in asset zero.

The objective is empirical upper-tail CVaR of actual unpaid amounts due by the horizon plus terminal cash-target shortfall. Future liabilities are reported separately and are never counted as already-missed payments. The required `future_liability_policy` is either `reserve_nominal_cash` (add their nominal balance to the terminal cash requirement) or `report_only` (report them without adding them to this horizon objective). Neither is an approved economic policy. Discounted reserve requirements require separately supplied assumptions, as in the source-linked client helper. Fractional tail weights avoid quantile tie inflation. Written-off amounts due by the horizon remain unpaid in the failure metric. Interim late payments are present in ledger receipts but the default objective does not impose an additional lateness penalty; a client requiring such a penalty needs a separate amendment. Only the first target should be considered at the next replan. No execution/trading adapter exists.

## Ledger contract and unresolved choices

Positions are units; prices are currency per unit; cash, liabilities, arrears, fees, payments and P&L are currency amounts. Decimal precision 40 is used internally. Holdings are marked units × prices. Capital gains change holdings, never cash. Cash dividends and cash interest are separate flows. No borrowing, shorting, lot rounding, taxes, bid/ask spread, settlement lag, interest on arrears, currency conversion, are modeled. Beginning-of-step external contributions are explicit uniquely identified cash-flow events, independent of returns. Such inputs require a new explicit contract.

Cash yield and return convention are required transition inputs, including explicit zero yield. Planner cash yields are a required horizon-length sequence. Each policy must specify all of:

- Payment funding: `cash_only` or `sell_pro_rata` (proportionate holdings liquidation net of fees).
- Unpaid obligation: `carry` as arrears or explicit `write_off` with retained payment failure.
- Timing: payment `before_trade` or `after_market`.
- Terminal treatment: `mark` or `liquidate` net of fees.
- Fee rate: nonnegative proportional charge strictly below one, applied to executed absolute dollar notional.

No one policy is selected as economically approved. Zero weights retain cash. On a rebalance, post-fee NAV V solves `V + c sum_i |w_i V - h_i| = pretrade_wealth`; a deterministic bounded bisection funds trades and fees together. Splits multiply units and divide prices before market returns. Price returns exclude split mechanics; dividends are specified per post-split share, with any ex-dividend price drop contained in the supplied price return. A total-return simulation prohibits additional splits or distributions to prevent double counting; its units are reinvested wealth units, not exchange-share inventory.

Step order: register liabilities and activate dues; credit explicit beginning-of-step contributions; optional before-trade payment; target rebalance; split; market return; cash dividend; cash interest; optional after-market payment; optional terminal liquidation. The two payment timings and corporate-action order are explicit supported conventions. Dues use `(due_at,id)` ordering. Obligations require globally unique IDs. Unpaid carried balances remain the same obligation, never a new charge. A terminal step closes the state and rejects further transitions. Terminal liquidation occurs after payments and does not retrospectively cure arrears; this is visible in receipts.

Conservation checks:

`ending gross wealth = starting gross wealth + contributions + market P&L + distributions + cash interest - fees - actual payments`

`ending equity = starting equity + contributions + market P&L + distributions + cash interest - fees - newly recognized liabilities + explicit write-offs`

Payment settles both asset cash and debt, so it does not reduce equity a second time. Receipts retain starting values, each payment's due/paid/unpaid/write-off amounts, turnover, fees, and complete ending state. Missing policies or liabilities are never invented.
