## Recommended investment architecture with available case facts

Use a liability-first USD portfolio. Price instruments that pay the ten $50,000 operating obligations by their respective beginning-year dates from 2033 through 2042. Investigate individual US Treasury principal/coupon strips or a cash-flow-matched Treasury ladder for this reserve, subject to actual access and execution terms. Maturities must occur on or before the obligation date; coupon reinvestment cannot be assumed at a fixed future rate. Short-term bill funds are useful liquidity candidates, but rolling them is not a locked 2033-2042 cash-flow match.

Only the surplus after reserve cost and a chosen flexibility buffer belongs in a growth sleeve. For that sleeve, a provisional diversified split of 60% broad US equity / 40% broad non-US equity is a simple policy starting point to review, not a model-optimized result or required competition weighting. VTI and VXUS are candidate implementations according to Vanguard's public product descriptions. They are not verified WInS-approved tickers. Do not add concentrated AI/technology stock bets on top without evaluating their overlap with broad-market holdings.

SGOV is a candidate for temporary USD liquidity, not the long-dated reserve itself. The issuer describes 0-3-month US Treasury-bill exposure. Its published 30-day SEC yield was 3.72% as of 8 October and expense ratio 0.09%; the yield is neither a promised future return nor a rate locked until 2042. The allocation analysis does not use that yield as a forecast.

## Dollar illustration, explicitly conditional

At the beginning of 2027 the case supplies $300,000; another $150,000 arrives at the beginning of 2028. Do not invest the future contribution today. With a hypothetical flat 4% discount curve, the 2027 value of the ten liabilities is $333,328. The present value of the later contribution is $144,231. If that later contribution is certain and can fund the remaining reserve at the assumed prices, the first contribution's reserve assignment is $189,097, leaving $110,903 before a flexibility buffer. Applying the provisional 60/40 growth split gives $66,542 US / $44,361 non-US.

This is a sensitivity illustration, NOT a trade ticket: no executable maturity-specific prices were supplied, the flat curve is an assumption, and the later contribution cannot be borrowed against or treated as already invested. At a hypothetical 2% curve, the first reserve assignment rises to $259,731 and surplus falls to $40,269. At 0%, the two contributions are $50,000 short even before facility spending. If future reserve prices cannot be locked, retain a margin or reduce growth rather than claiming certainty.

No overall percentage recommendation is certified until actual reserve costs, the flexibility buffer, holdings and team policy are known. Facility spending should be residual and conditional; the current case does not justify a guaranteed dollar promise in 2031.
