# Why the retained FREM run loses

FREM's main measurable failure is a severely miscentered return forecast. Memory changes the training trajectory, but its final correction is too small to explain the observed gap directly. The saved correction actually helps slightly. The available evidence identifies the numerical failure and several plausible upstream mechanisms; it cannot identify every training cause without new controlled development experiments.

This is a **post-hoc descriptive audit of one synthetic development run**, not a market result, a refit, a new hypothesis test, or a repair selected on test outcomes. Run: `outputs/engineering_yahoo_calibrated_v1/20261011T110103Z_a65733448e`. Scope: four synthetic assets, seed 11, 289 training dates / 1,156 training rows, 91 validation dates, 92 test dates / 368 paired test rows, two neural epochs. `diagnosis.json` contains reproducible values, retained histories and source hashes. `additional_descriptive_checks.json` retains the additional ungated-residual calculation without selecting it.

| FREM | -0.901362 | 0.053865 | -0.045884 | 0.004389 |
| Separately trained no-memory | -1.466289 | 0.041766 | 0.031799 | 0.004389 |
| Ridge | -1.691472 | 0.033535 | 0.005597 | 0.004389 |
| XGBoost | -1.790440 | 0.030654 | 0.005654 | 0.004389 |

Negative log likelihood can be negative for continuous densities. FREM's less-negative score is worse. These are cumulative five-executable-interval log returns, not portfolio profits or simple percentage returns.

1. **Demonstrated: a large downward location bias.** Every FREM test forecast is negative. Its mean forecast is -4.5884% in log-return units versus an actual mean of +0.4389%, a -5.0273 percentage-point error. The squared overall bias accounts for 63.12% of its MSE. The bias and NLL disadvantage occur in all four assets, rather than arising from one bad ticker. No-memory is also biased, but in the opposite direction and less severely. The simple baselines have much smaller mean errors. This diagnoses the dominant failure of this saved predictor; it does not establish why optimization learned that bias.

2. **Demonstrated: its probability distribution is poorly calibrated around that wrong mean.** Nominal 95% intervals cover only 76.90% of outcomes, and 209/368 observations fall in the top tenth of the predictive CDF. Its directional Brier score is 0.44125. Replacing saved distribution fields between the two neural models in all eight possible combinations attributes 78.79% of their 0.564927-nat NLL gap to location, 20.62% to scale, and 0.59% to degrees of freedom, averaged across replacement orders. This is an exact arithmetic decomposition, not a causal training attribution. It does not recommend a scale fitted to these outcomes. FREM's mean Student-t scale is 0.02889, which is not its standard deviation; miscentering itself contributes to poor coverage. Simply inflating uncertainty would leave the bias unresolved.

3. **Demonstrated: the direct memory correction is not the main source of this loss.** At the same FREM checkpoint, scoring `base_prediction` gives NLL -0.898507; the deployed corrected prediction gives -0.901362. Thus memory improves the score by 0.002855 nats on these saved rows. Mean absolute correction is only 0.000180 log-return units (1.80 basis points), compared with a 0.050273 mean forecast error. The average effective gate is 0.008037, and the selected global controller weight is 0.018384. Retrieval succeeds with four neighbors on every test row. This rules out 'no retrieval occurred' and contradicts 'turning off the final correction recovers the no-memory baseline.' The baseline was separately trained.

4. **Demonstrated conditions, unresolved causality: the two-epoch training budget produces very different selected predictors.** Both neural variants have 20,170 trainable parameters, identical saved scalers, the same seed and training settings, and only `use_memory` differs in model configuration. Each executes 38 optimizer updates. FREM selects epoch 1 (after 19 updates): validation NLL -1.02765, worsening to -0.92615 in epoch 2 despite lower training loss. No-memory selects epoch 2: validation NLL improves from -0.68315 to -1.65932. This is substantial trajectory/checkpoint sensitivity, not convergence evidence. Both stop at the requested epoch budget, not the wall limit. The controller needs two bad validations to reduce the learning rate, so neither run exercises that adaptation. More epochs might help or hurt; the existing test cannot decide that.

5. **Source-supported concern: the readout has a sensitive optimization scale.** The mean is an unconstrained linear readout multiplied by horizon 5, while the scale uses softplus and square-root-horizon scaling. Inputs are robust-scaled; return targets are not standardized. Recorded pre-clipping global gradient norms average 553.3 and 443.1 for FREM's two epochs, versus a clipping threshold of 1.0. No-memory also has large norms (711.7 and 531.7), so clipping alone does not explain the relative loss. The facts justify measuring head-wise gradients, location drift, clip frequency and training-only scaling controls. They do not prove an optimizer bug, a specific better learning rate, or divergence. Source: `src/frem/model.py` return-head initialization/forward; `src/frem/training.py` `_gradient_step`.

6. **Source-supported concern: memory residuals are defined against an evolving, dropout-active predictor.** Training queues `outcome - base_prediction` as forecasts occur, updates model weights between chronological batches, and retains the resulting mixed residuals. It does not rebuild values against the selected final predictor. The fit report explicitly calls these 'training-trajectory base forecasts; fixed keys, evolving readout residuals.' Among 128 retained records, the historical base predictions range from -0.05022 to +0.00995 and average -0.02562. These are not estimates of how the final predictor would err on the same histories. Dropout is active during training and disabled at evaluation, adding another mismatch. This can make corrections stale or noisy; causality and magnitude require a predeclared frozen-base or chronological out-of-fold residual control. Reusing the same training rows with a final in-sample fit would itself need careful bias analysis.

7. **Source-supported concern: the controller and memory utility judge different actions and objectives.** The global controller compares squared error of `base + full residual` against the base. Record utility compares squared error of the actually gated prediction against the base. The neural head and primary evaluation optimize Student-t NLL. A full correction that overshoots may be penalized even when a small gated correction helps; an MSE improvement need not improve NLL. At the selected checkpoint the controller is near its minimum weight, but the final tiny correction still helps on these rows. The separate full-residual arithmetic is also preserved; it improves this known sample but remains worse than the independently trained no-memory model and is **not** permission to tune the gate on this sample. Source: `src/frem/controller.py:observe_matured`, `src/frem/memory.py:_credit`, `src/frem/model.py:forward`.

8. **Demonstrated design conditions, unproven harm: finite capacity and frozen evaluation limit adaptation.** The run writes all 1,156 matured episodes, filters none, evicts 1,028 and retains 128 (30–33 per asset). Forty-eight retained episodes have never received utility credit. The count of 244 credits describes surviving records only, not total lifetime credit or independent evidence. All retained records are 136–185 calendar days old at the first test decision and 265–314 days old at the last. Validation and test use `online_update=false`, so no recent outcomes refresh them. This is the declared frozen test policy, not an execution failure. Same-asset retrieval and four neighbors limit the candidate pool. Its harm is unknown: stale episodes need not be intrinsically worse in this stationary synthetic generator. FIFO/random, capacity and online-policy controls were not run.

9. **Demonstrated generator mismatch: this fixture contains no planted temporal return predictability.** `scripts/generate_yahoo_calibrated_engineering_panel.py` draws independent multivariate-normal daily return vectors with fixed moments, then compounds prices. Cross-asset contemporaneous covariance is present, but changing regimes, recurring predictive episodes and serial return signal are not planted. Conditional future expected return is constant per asset. Flexible retrieval, spectral features and JEPA cannot extract a temporal signal that the generator does not supply. This is a useful null/engineering setting: the model should avoid inventing a strong directional bias. It is not a positive test of the claimed recurring-memory mechanism, and it cannot establish that the mechanism fails in markets.

10. **Source-supported concern: auxiliary-loss improvement is not evidence of return skill.** FREM's JEPA loss falls from 0.03364 to 0.01243 while validation NLL worsens. Future representation targets include rolling volatility and momentum features that partly reuse returns already present in the context. Even with independent new returns, these summaries can persist. Improving representation prediction can therefore occur without discovering future return information. The regularizer contribution to total loss is small, but its gradient effect was not measured. There is no no-JEPA/spectral/reservoir control in this four-cell run, and nonzero latent variance does not establish full-rank useful representations. Do not attribute this result to 'JEPA collapse' or condemn a specialist branch without its control. Source: `src/frem/data.py:_feature_frame`, `build_windows`; `src/frem/model.py:encode_target`, `representation_losses`.

11. **Demonstrated comparison difference: simple baselines receive an explicit uncertainty-calibration pass.** Ridge and XGBoost estimate Student-t scale from a purged chronological training tail, then refit their point predictor on all training rows. FREM jointly learns mean, scale and degrees of freedom and selects by validation NLL; it has no corresponding separate calibration pass. This helps explain what differs in the comparison, but the baselines also have lower MAE, so their advantage cannot be dismissed as only a likelihood-calibration artifact. Neural vs neural is the cleaner memory comparison because their procedures match.

12. **Evidence limitation, not an identified loss cause: this is one short path and one optimization seed.** Overlapping five-interval targets, contemporaneously correlated assets and repeated contexts reduce independent information. The retained block-bootstrap result is unchanged: control minus FREM NLL -0.564927; 95% interval [-1.132572, -0.002588]; centered two-sided p = 0.054726, 200 resamples and 20-date blocks. Percentile intervals and a separately centered bootstrap test need not agree at the 5% threshold. No new p-value, favorable block choice, omitted seed or market significance claim is introduced here. The six mechanism controls and broader baseline family remain unexecuted. Current market cells remain at zero, so data admission/resource limitations prevent learning more; they are not explanations for this already completed synthetic loss.

The older 2026-10-08 synthetic amendment separately recorded memory MSE 9.2696% worse than its own memory-off control. Its code, objective and data differ; it is not another independent FREM replication. The repaired legacy report contains leakage demonstrations and null findings. Those artifacts remain preserved, and none were reopened as a spent evaluation or used to tune this version.

The next discriminating development work, once admitted, should measure training trajectories and output calibration first, then isolate residual reference and controller-objective changes one at a time. Declare a fresh null/positive-control generator family and complete seed budget before running it, with constant/zero and existing strong baselines. Preserve the old negative result. Compare frozen-base, chronologically cross-fitted or truly delayed-online residuals under explicit information contracts, and test full/gated controller credit under a common objective. Separate calibration must use training/validation only. Add matched no-JEPA, no-spectral and no-reservoir controls before assigning blame to those branches. A positive-control generator must have predeclared known recurrence; it must not be engineered after outcomes to guarantee a win. These are proposed tests, not completed experiments or an assertion that FREM can be made to win.
