## Prior art verified from primary sources

1. **Decision and forecast horizons.** Gârleanu and Pedersen derive a cost-aware policy that depends on existing holdings and signal decay. Their assumptions and commodity-futures empirical setting do not establish that S8's one-week coefficients persist for four intervals. A horizon-matched target or a validated path model is required to test that assumption. [NBER working paper, issued August 2009, revised December 2013](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15205).
2. **Optimization is an established decision layer.** Boyd et al. distinguish forecast generation from convex single- and multi-period trading and explain the approximations used. The framework can exploit forecasts; its existence supplies no evidence that FREM's forecasts are informative. Reusing it is an implementation choice, not a new algorithmic contribution. [Authors' paper page, August 2017](https://stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/cvx_portfolio.html).
3. **Selection can overfit.** Cawley and Talbot show that noise in a selection criterion can produce performance differences comparable to the differences between algorithms. Feature, expert, calibration and policy selection therefore belong inside chronological development; the best S8 secondary pair cannot become the primary retroactively. [JMLR, 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v11/cawley10a.html).
4. **Uncertainty has an estimand.** Bates, Hastie and Tibshirani distinguish performance of a particular fitted model from average performance of a fitting procedure, and show why naive cross-validation intervals can under-cover. Their results are not a turnkey validity proof for this dependent market series. Here, uncertainty conditional on stored forecasts excludes model fitting and procedure selection; an honest procedure-level claim must evaluate those operations inside the outer temporal design. [Authors' paper, first posted 2021, published JASA 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00673).
5. **Simple allocation controls are serious controls.** DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal found that none of fourteen estimators consistently exceeded 1/N across their seven datasets under their metrics. This is evidence for retaining equal-weight and simple risk controls; it is not a theorem that 1/N always wins, and their sample-size calculations cannot be transplanted to three ETFs. [Publisher abstract, online 2007, volume 2009](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/22/5/1915/1592901).
6. **Student-t tails constrain the economic interpretation.** NIST and SciPy give the polynomial-tail density and location-scale transformation. The exponential-moment result below follows directly from that density; it is a derivation in this review, not a quotation of an empirical finance result. [NIST t distribution](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda3664.htm), [SciPy official t documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.t.html).

Sources were checked on 14 October 2026. No priority, novelty, profitability or universal no-regret claim is established by this review.

## Mathematical boundary

For a nondegenerate Student-t log return L with any finite positive degrees of freedom nu, its upper-tail density is asymptotic to C l^(-nu-1), where C>0. Therefore integral exp(l) f(l) dl over sufficiently large positive l diverges. In particular E[exp(L)] is infinite, even if nu>2 makes Var(L) finite. The Gaussian conversion exp(mu+variance/2)-1 is not the expected simple return of this law. Numerical truncation creates a different law and must be specified as such.

This does **not** make every expected utility under Student-t log returns undefined. If a long-only portfolio retains a strictly positive cash floor, log portfolio wealth is bounded below and its upper tail is bounded by a constant plus a sum of positive log returns. For nu>1 those first absolute moments are finite. Thus exact expected log utility may exist, but marginal log moments alone do not specify it: cross-asset dependence, holding horizon, costs and the full joint law matter. A cash floor also does not by itself secure dated liabilities.

The implemented S8 policy deliberately optimizes H sum_i(mu_i w_i - 4 v_i w_i^2) minus costs. It is a concave surrogate with constant multiweek coefficients and diagonal risk, not an exact expectation. The S6 scenario generator samples Student-t shocks in **simple-return** space and floors outcomes at -1, with an optional default/jump component. Flooring/jumps change the resulting moments. A successor must estimate and diagnose the actual output distribution; pre-floor inputs are not post-floor means and covariances.

## Recommendation

Complete the retained-evidence diagnosis and the negative/inconclusive research package now. Keep the fixed ensemble as the predictive incumbent and simple passive/risk controls as economic incumbents. Do not adopt the retrospectively favorable ridge/H1 pair. The next defensible research question is whether **one directly horizon-matched, uncertainty-shrunk return forecast can improve net economic performance over simple controls under the same risk budget**, before attempting to recover the original memory claim. The material difference is the economic target and complete selection procedure, not a renamed S8 or a weaker support threshold.
