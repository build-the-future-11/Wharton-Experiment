## Strategy summary

Our proposed decision process begins with the client's documented objectives and obligations. It separates cash needed for payments from capital that can bear investment risk. We compare transparent portfolio rules against a fully invested reference, retain downside failures, and record the purpose and evidence behind each security decision. We will not rely on the laboratory's earlier forecasting claim: it was withdrawn after leakage was found. A portfolio recommendation remains conditional on the actual client case, eligible instruments and risk constraints.

The laboratory contains twelve implemented models, but implementation is distinct from investment usefulness. ## Alternatives and competitive landscape

## Economics and financial assumptions

Venture revenue and customer unit economics are not applicable to this competition. The relevant economics are asset returns, liquidity, trading costs, inflation and obligations. The calculator normalizes starting wealth to one, models hypothetical sleeves and tests explicitly assumed return paths. It records transaction costs, payments, unpaid obligations and remaining purchasing power. It does not estimate client wealth, expected market returns, security fees, taxes or real execution costs. Costs and reserve policy must be recalibrated from the actual case and applicable trading rules before making a recommendation.

## Implementation and adoption

First, students ingest the Apply materials and annotate source facts. Next, they set an eligible universe and justify a strategic allocation with an explicit benchmark. They test obligations and downside risk, then decide whether any additional model improves the decision enough to justify its complexity. Once authorized within the competition, they record WInS decisions and exact notes as executed, review deviations, and use the final report to explain what changed and why. No outreach to the competition client is proposed.

## Impact and success measures

Proposed measures are source completeness, percentage of holdings with a documented client rationale, funded obligations under stated assumptions, risk and cost exposure, and traceable changes in reasoning. These are proposed tracking measures, not achieved outcomes. Simulator returns, client outcomes and competition placement are unobserved. A funding stress failure is a reason to reconsider spending, contributions, horizon or risk, not to choose a more flattering scenario.

## Risk and governance

Principal risks are missing or misread client facts, unsupported return assumptions, illiquid or ineligible instruments, inflation, losses near withdrawals, model leakage, transaction costs and overstatement of student authorship. Required controls are source references, an explicit information clock, comparisons with simple baselines, preserved negative results, a decision log and student review. No client funding guarantee is made.
